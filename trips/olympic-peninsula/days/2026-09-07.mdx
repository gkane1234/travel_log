We check out of the hotel, with the desire to do upright paddle boarding as my ankle was hurting a I didn’t think I could do a hike that day. We stopped by a small breakfast place that was manned by a single woman with filler and botox. We got to Lake crescent to do it and the paddleboard were booked out until later in the day. We walked around Crescent lake for a while exploring the old growth forest there. After a while we went back and found out that all activities were suspended because it was too windy. We decided then to go to Ruby beach on the western edge of the peninsula instead of trying to do SUP for the first time on rough waters later in the day.

We drove to Ruby Beach the drive was pretty interesting, we passed through Forks just before the annual Twilight festival. The beach itself was highly rugged and beautiful. Covered in drift wood. 

We immediately tried to climb a big rock and I almost got stuck and thought I was going to die. Julia then climbed up to where I aborted my ascent and, although I pleaded, continued up higher. We then spent about 5 minutes trying to get Julia back down, pleading with our higher power that this would be our last time doing such things. We got down to the ground and swore off our daredevil ways. 



![img-5170-2](/trip-media/olympic-peninsula/photos/img-5170-2.jpg)


We then walked around the beach a bit more and found a huge vertical rock-island thing and tried to navigate along the edge of it that was in contact with the ocean.



To get there I surmised the only way to get onto the rocks was by climbing through what we eventually dubbed the birth canal, as it was a narrow passage through the jagged rocks whose floor was covered in unsettling intertidal sealife that looked squishy and toxic. 


<TripVideo src="/trip-media/olympic-peninsula/photos/img-5173.mp4" />


After passing through I immediately found a path that seemed to go onto the top of the massive wooded stone hill. It was extremely steep and we needed to hold onto roots and rocks in order to ascend. Eventually we decided that we were in too precarious of a position to continue but took a few further steps to see if it became more passable. Once we bailed we realized our divine perjury and had to take our survival of this descent into our mortal hands. After making it back down, we continued climbing on other large rocks to pose for more sick shots.

Afterwards a group of a few white dudes came by and I saw one trying to scale the mount and so I directed his attention to what I believed to be the path we had taken before. As they started to climb I realized that this was not the path that we had taken and instead was a much more precarious path. I had to go back and watch them as they bailed and helped them descend with their fates now seemingly on my hands. Once they made it down we walked back and introduced ourselves. They came from tennessee and pennsylvania, and I sort of assumed them to be some hard republican dude bros. Anyways after they mentioned that there was a dead whale at a nearby beach and they were headed there. We immediately decided to go along. After a 10 minute drive we arrived at the next beach and walked down together. The excitement of seeing a dead whale captivated us all and we started to run towards where we were told the whale was. The dead whale was not recently deceased and had an absolutely prutrid odor.

We spoke to the guys some more to find out they were mostly zoologists who worked at the nashville zoo and one had a shirt that said “queer icon” on it. I immediately felt a lot more at home. We asked many questions about the whale, and then the ethereal moon jellyfish that had washed up as well.
